# Special Chapter — Mother's Tears

This place was called the Chaos-Realm.

At least, that was what Lara had heard her father call it for as long as she could remember. To her, that world was not a collection of explanations about Mana, spatial rifts, or disputes between nations. It was Wavery, with its familiar streets and the palace where she lived with her parents—a place where she could run until her legs ached and still wish she had more time to play.

---

"Mother! I'm going out to play for a while!"

Her voice rang through the great kitchen of the royal palace.

"All right, sweetheart. Be careful!"

The woman standing beside the stove turned around. Seeing her daughter about to run off, she set down her ladle, walked over, and gently caught Lara by the shoulder.

"Not so fast."

"Yes?"

Mio bent down, kissed Lara on the forehead, and smoothed her slightly disheveled hair.

"You can go and play, but remember to come home for dinner. I'm making your favorite today."

"Really?!"

"Really. If you're home on time."

"Then I'll definitely be back!"

Lara threw her arms around her mother before finally running off. Mio watched her disappear through the doorway, one hand still raised as though she wanted to wave just once more.

"Your Majesty, please leave this work to us!"

An elderly cook sounded almost desperate when he saw her pick up the ladle again.

"There is no need for the Queen herself to cook. Or is it that Your Majesty doesn't trust our skills?"

"No, of course not!" Mio waved both hands. "I trust you. It's just that I need a little exercise too. I can't sit around all day, especially when I've still got this much energy!"

The woman was **Mio**, Queen of Wavery. Even after years of palace life, she still enjoyed preparing meals with her own hands, laughing loudly when something amused her, and occasionally bewildering the servants even more than the young princess did.

They were used to it by now. A few sighed and returned to their tasks, but none of them truly wanted to stop her anymore.

---

Lara ran along the paved road leading away from the palace, cheerfully greeting guards and familiar townspeople as she passed. Her destination was the same as always: the forest near the palace grounds, where she often slipped away to play despite repeated instructions to tell an adult first.

The palace staff had tried forbidding her from going.

Lara had listened like a perfectly obedient child. Then, a few days later, she had found another way out.

So, rather than spend their days chasing a princess who regarded every unexplored corner as an invitation, they had entrusted her safety to someone capable of bringing them both home if danger arose.

Just as Lara was about to turn down an alley, someone snapped their fingers.

*Snap!*

"Huh?!"

She stopped short and whirled around.

"Princess."

A young girl emerged from the shade beside a stone wall. She wore an expression far more serious than her years suggested.

"You shouldn't go out alone. At least let me accompany you."

"No, no! You've got a hundred things to do! Aren't you supposed to be training with your father today?"

"Princess, I may only be thirteen, but I have been assigned to protect you."

"I never said you couldn't do it!"

"Besides, Rifts have been appearing more frequently outside Wavery. His Majesty erected a barrier to prevent them from forming within our borders, but that doesn't mean we can afford to be careless."

Lara puffed out her cheeks. She had heard the warning so many times that she could recite it by heart.

"I just want to play for a little while…"

"And I just want to make sure you make it home for dinner."

At the mention of dinner, Lara hesitated for a few seconds before letting out a long sigh.

"Fine! Then you shall escort me today, **Kara**!"

"As you wish. After all, I am a full two years older than Princess **Lara**. It's only natural that I understand certain necessities better than you."

"You talk too much!"

Lara seized Kara's hand and tried to drag her along. Kara gently held her back.

"One moment."

She pressed her palm against the stone wall by the exit. For an instant, a faint Mana mark appeared beneath her fingertips, then sank into the stone and vanished as if it had never existed.

"What was that for?" Lara tilted her head.

"Making sure we have a way home."

Kara withdrew her hand. She had established a teleportation anchor—a point she could return to by activating her ability. She was not yet skilled at prolonged combat, but she was confident that, so long as she could reach Lara, she could get the princess out of danger.

"There. Let's go."

"Finally!"

The two girls ran toward the forest, still holding hands.

---

The sky of the Chaos-Realm bore its distinctive deep-crimson hue. Mutated creatures, peoples of unusual appearance, and regions affected by unstable Mana had long been part of everyday life there.

Among the many nations in that world, **Wavery** was considered relatively peaceful under the rule of King **Lerion**. Its settlements, along with the forest Lara loved to visit, lay within the protective barrier he maintained himself.

To a child like Lara, that meant the forest was simply the forest: somewhere to pick flowers, climb trees, and race Kara across broad clearings.

She had no reason to think today would be any different.

---

Back at the palace, Lerion passed the kitchen and spotted a familiar figure by the fire.

"As energetic as ever, aren't you, Mio?"

The Queen turned, resting one hand on her hip.

"What can I say? I warned you about that before we married."

"I remember."

"Which is why you should never have married a commoner like—"

Lerion stepped forward and gently pinched her cheek before she could finish.

"Ow! What was that for?"

"I never said there was anything I disliked about it." He released her cheek and looked at the blush spreading across her face. "Whenever I see you in the kitchen, it feels just like when we first fell in love."

"You…"

Mio lowered her head. Even the tips of her ears were turning red.

"Don't say things that make me blush like that."

The servants exchanged discreet glances and found excuses to leave. Soon, the spacious kitchen held little more than the crackle of the fire and the conversation of a married couple.

"Oh, that reminds me," Lerion said. "Where's Lara?"

"Out playing. I told her to come home for dinner."

"Again?" He shook his head, smiling despite himself. "That girl never sits still. Just like her mother."

"And what's wrong with that? Perhaps she'll also inherit her father's wisdom and good judgment one day."

The compliment made Lerion look momentarily bashful. Mio turned off the stove, stepped closer, stood on tiptoe, and kissed his cheek.

He looked at her for a moment, then spoke as though the thought had only just occurred to him.

"What do you think about… having another child?"

"Oh, you!"

Mio gave his shoulder a light punch, her face even redder than before.

"All right, enough of that. You set the table. I'll go find Lara."

She turned to leave, but Lerion caught her hand.

"Hm?"

He drew her close and kissed her on the lips. When he finally pulled away, Mio stood frozen for several seconds.

"Be careful out there."

"I… I know!"

She hurried away, trying to hide her burning face. Lerion could not help laughing.

At that very moment, someone knocked at the door.

"Your Majesty. The council is waiting. A matter of national security requires your immediate attention."

Lerion turned. The messenger bowed, holding out a sealed report with both hands.

"Is it truly that urgent?"

"Yes, Your Majesty. All the relevant officials have been summoned."

Lerion accepted the document. He skimmed the first few lines, then glanced toward the doorway Mio had just passed through.

"Very well. I'll be there at once."

He did not know that, elsewhere in the kingdom, people who had prepared for a very long time were waiting for an opportunity that would last only a few minutes.

---

Deep in the forest, Lara and Kara found a clearing where flowers grew beneath the trees.

"Princess, what are you going to do with all those flowers?" Kara asked, picking one with dark-colored petals.

Lara gave her a mischievous smile.

"You're a girl too, you know, but you haven't got a feminine bone in your body!"

"…What does that have to do with my question?"

"Hmph! I'm going to take them home as a surprise for Mother!"

"Oh." Kara studied the flowers and nodded. "I see. As for being feminine… I think the princess has enough of that for both of us. There's no need for me to change."

"You…!"

Lara wanted to argue, but one look at Kara's perfectly serious face left her at a loss.

"Whatever! Hurry up! We still have to get back before dinner!"

"Yes, Princess."

A deep-red flower caught Lara's eye. Its petals reminded her of her mother's hair, so she carefully set it apart from the others.

Not far from the two girls, a ripple of light passed through the air and disappeared.

Kara looked up. It had felt like a faint fluctuation of Mana, but when she examined the area, the forest remained peaceful. The familiar presence of Wavery's barrier seemed entirely normal.

She did not know that a thin layer of interference was concealing the gradual erosion of the real barrier. From hiding places beyond its protected perimeter, watchers had been waiting for this exact moment.

About ten meters to Lara's left, the air began to crack.

First, a hairline fracture.

Then another.

Neither girl noticed in time.

---

"That girl… She's probably run into the forest again."

Mio murmured to herself as she walked. She knew almost all of Lara's favorite places, especially the clearing full of flowers. The thought of her daughter coming home with both hands covered in dirt made her smile.

Then she remembered her husband's kiss and unconsciously touched her lips.

"Honestly… He never gives me any warning."

She went farther into the trees.

"Lara! Where are—"

Her call broke off.

Beyond the trunks ahead, an enormous tear split open the air. A violent wind rose, dragging soil, rocks, and branches toward the widening darkness.

Mio froze for a heartbeat.

"…Lara?"

Then she sprinted forward so fast that the branches on either side were still shaking after she had passed.

---

"LARA!"

Kara screamed.

The fracture, no more than a thin line at first, had expanded at a terrifying speed. Small bushes were ripped from the ground, and the flowers the girls had gathered scattered in the wind. Kara dug her heels into the earth and tried to pull Lara backward, but the pull grew stronger with every passing second.

*Impossible.*

This forest was inside the barrier His Majesty had created. If a Rift could appear here, then…

For an instant, Kara thought of someone—someone who might know far more about Wavery's barrier than they should. She clenched her jaw and pushed the suspicion aside. Suspicions would not save Lara now.

"GIVE ME YOUR HAND!"

The girls had been separated. Lara clung to a tangle of exposed roots with both hands, her feet dangling in the howling wind.

"Ka… Kara!"

Kara fought her way closer. The anchor she had placed on the wall outside the palace was still there. All she needed was to touch Lara and hold the connection for a single instant. Then she could bring them both back.

But just as their fingertips came within reach, something flashed past Kara's face.

*Whoosh!*

An arrow struck the earth exactly where she had been standing.

"What…?"

Another came. Kara twisted aside and drew the short sword at her hip. Farther among the trees, two cloaked figures were raising bows. The wind did not sweep them off their feet as it did the trees around them. Beneath each attacker, a thin field of Mana enveloped their boots and the ground, holding them firmly in place.

The protective fields trembled under the Rift's influence, but they did not dissolve.

"ENEMIES!"

Kara shouted and looked for another way to reach Lara. The next arrow flew not at her, but straight toward the princess. Kara had no choice but to leap across its path. Her short sword knocked the arrow aside, though the impact sent a painful shock through her arm.

"LARA!"

Another voice rang out behind them.

"MOTHER!"

Mio burst into the clearing and saw her daughter struggling at the edge of the pull. She did not stop to find out who the cloaked figures were. She ran straight for Lara.

An arrow buried itself in her calf.

"Ngh…!"

Mio dropped to one knee, striking the ground hard. She gritted her teeth, forced herself up, and dragged her injured leg forward.

"MOTHER!"

"Don't let go, Lara! I'm coming!"

Kara turned toward the two archers. A third figure emerged from the opposite edge of the forest and charged at Mio. He wore the same strange Mana-infused boots; every step held firm against the Rift's tremendous pull.

"YOUR MAJESTY!"

Kara rushed to intercept him with her short sword. The attacker was forced to change direction, but the two archers seized the opening behind her.

*Thud!*

An arrow sank deep into Kara's abdomen.

"…Ghk!"

She staggered, clutching the wound. Her legs threatened to give way, but she fought to remain standing, keeping her blade between the assassin and the mother and daughter.

Her father had trained her, yet this battle was far beyond her experience. Kara was better at preparing escape routes than fighting several enemies at once in a region of unstable space. Her anchor was still waiting on the palace wall. If she activated it, she could at least save herself.

But Lara was still there.

And so was Mio.

Kara knew she could not bear the thought of abandoning them.

"Just… a little longer…"

She tried to hold her position in the archers' line of fire, but pain and blood loss were already blurring her sight.

Ahead, Mio finally reached Lara. She knelt and wrapped both arms around her daughter, shielding the little girl with her back and shoulders.

"Mother! I'm sorry! I… I…"

Lara sobbed. Almost all the flowers she had treasured had been swept away. Only a few crumpled stems remained in her trembling hand.

"It's all right, sweetheart." Mio pressed Lara's head against her chest. "I'm here now."

Another arrow pierced Mio's shoulder.

Her body jerked. A strange numbness spread through her arm, then down across her chest and into the fingertips still holding her daughter.

"…Poison?"

Mio looked toward the attackers. Beneath their cloaks, they remained standing as though they had known exactly how to prepare for an ambush beside a Rift.

Farther away, Kara reached toward the mother and daughter one last time.

"Prin… cess…"

Her short sword slipped from her fingers. The young guard collapsed among the uprooted roots.

---

In the council chamber, Lerion turned to the next page of the report. The assembled officials were debating a matter of border security, supposedly serious enough to require a decision from the King himself.

Everything written on the pages sounded plausible. Yet Lerion could find nothing so urgent that it justified summoning an emergency meeting without warning.

He was about to speak when his fingers stopped moving.

An uneasy feeling settled in his chest.

Lerion turned toward the window. Wavery's barrier still felt as stable as ever, but something about the space near the forest Lara often visited troubled him.

"Your Majesty?"

He sprang to his feet.

"Postpone the meeting."

"But Your Majesty, this report—"

Space twisted around Lerion. Before anyone could finish speaking, he was gone.

---

The Rift's pull grew more violent by the second. Mio held Lara against her chest, one hand clinging to the last roots that had not yet been torn from the earth. Her wounded leg throbbed, and the poison was making it harder to move.

"Lara."

"Y-yes…?"

"Look at me."

The girl lifted her head. Mio managed her familiar smile, although she had no idea how much longer they could endure this.

"Don't be afraid."

"You won't let go of me either, will you, Mother…?"

"I won't let go."

At that moment, the air beside them warped.

"MIO!"

Lerion appeared in the raging wind. When he saw the arrow in his wife's shoulder and Lara's hands clinging desperately to her, he wasted no time asking questions. He rushed forward and gathered them both into his arms.

"Hold on to me!"

Mana erupted around him. Lerion fixed his mind on a point near the palace, where he had been only moments earlier, and opened a passage through space.

A thin seam of light appeared.

*Crack!*

It shattered before it grew wide enough for even one person to pass through.

Lerion tried again. The moment he split open space, it collapsed in on itself, as though the nearby Rift were crushing every other passage he tried to create. Mana reaching into the chaotic region stretched thin and vanished.

"…Damn it."

He tried to stabilize the space directly around Mio and Lara instead. That protection lasted only a moment; fresh fractures tore through it almost at once.

Long-range Magic could not function this close to the Rift. He could not carve a way through it. Lerion knew the coordinates and could manipulate the space around him, but every method he attempted was broken apart by the chaos before him.

All he could do was hold on with his own strength, planting his feet on the remaining solid ground and trying to drag them out.

The earth cracked. Roots burst free. Still, Lerion refused to release them.

"Lerion…"

Mio looked at her husband. She could see exactly what was happening: each time he managed to pull them a little farther, the Rift widened and stole the distance back. If this went on, he would be dragged in as well.

"You can't stay here."

"I'm not letting go."

"You still have Wavery. And people who are waiting for you to come back."

"Mio, this isn't the time—"

"Then…" Mio glanced down at their daughter before looking up again. "Please take care of Lara for me."

Lerion went still.

He understood what she meant. If Lara could be separated from her mother's embrace, he would try to pull their daughter out of the danger zone first, then find some way to return for his wife.

But Lara had heard every word.

The girl jerked her head up to stare at Mio. Her eyes widened, then slowly moved to her father. There was fear in them, hesitation, and a question Lara did not know how to put into words.

Lerion had always loved his daughter. He knew she liked to run and play; he knew Mio often had to go looking for her before dinner. But he had also missed too many afternoons, too many conversations, and too many occasions when Lara had simply wanted her father to stay home a little longer.

Lara did not hate him. She was simply used to running to her mother whenever she was frightened. Now, faced with the thought of releasing Mio and entering the arms of a father who was so rarely beside her, she panicked.

"I… don't…"

Her fingers tightened around her mother's clothes.

"I don't want to leave Mother!"

Mio felt her daughter trembling as she clung to her. Lerion felt it too. For one brief instant, he looked at Lara as though he had only now realized how great the distance between them had become.

Just an instant.

But the Rift waited for no one.

The ground beneath Lerion's feet gave way again. Mio looked at him, then at Lara. If this continued, all three would disappear, leaving Wavery without its ruler.

She raised a hand that was rapidly losing its feeling and placed it against her husband's chest.

"I'm sorry."

"Mio…?"

"Don't let our people suffer through anything more."

Mio put what little strength she had left into a sudden push. Lerion was already off balance, still caught in the shock of his daughter's expression. His arms slipped away from them, and the force of the push sent him tumbling onto the ground at the outer edge of the pull.

"MIO!"

He caught himself and tried to rush back at once. But the ground he had occupied was crumbling, and the Rift widened for the last time, like a wound being torn open.

"LARA!"

Mio had time only to hold her daughter tighter.

Then both of them vanished into the darkness.

Space closed behind them.

---

Lerion rushed to the place where the Rift had been. His outstretched hand met nothing but empty air.

He poured Mana into the surrounding space, searching for a trace, a coordinate, anything that might connect him to wherever Mio and Lara had been taken.

There was nothing.

Without knowing their destination, he had no point to lock onto. For the first time in many years, the power that had carried him across impossible distances was utterly useless.

"Mio…"

He called his wife's name, his voice turning hoarse.

"Lara…"

No one answered.

An old voice suddenly echoed through his memory, so distant that he had thought he had forgotten it:

*"What good are you as a king, Lerion, if you can't even protect your own family?"*

He clenched his trembling fist. His Mana had not yet settled after coming so close to the Rift. Even a fraction of the pressure leaking from him unconsciously was enough to crack the ground beneath his feet.

Reinforcements finally arrived.

Among the broken trees, the cloaked attackers had not all managed to escape. Lerion spotted them. Space warped around him, and in the next instant he stood before the nearest one.

The fight ended in a heartbeat. One attacker was knocked down; the other two were disarmed. When Wavery's soldiers moved to restrain them, one bit down on something hidden in his mouth. His body convulsed and fell still. The other collapsed before he could utter a word of testimony.

Lerion crouched beside one of the bodies. The fallen cloak revealed an emblem embroidered on its inner lining: a Rift, its branching cracks seeming to tear space into pieces.

He recognized it.

"…Ritton."

That nation had sent envoys to Wavery to discuss cooperation and friendly relations between neighbors. Their proposals had sounded cordial, but Lerion had understood the ambition beneath them: to bring Wavery under Ritton's influence. He had seen this emblem during one of their earlier meetings.

Displaying it during a covert attack was hardly the act of someone desperate to avoid detection.

It was more like a provocation.

Lerion ground his teeth. Wavery lacked the military power to rush recklessly into a war. Ordinary people would be the first to pay for it. He knew that, even as his rage made both hands shake.

"Mio…"

He turned back toward the clearing where the Rift had closed. There was no trace of his wife or daughter. The trees nearest him began to split under the Mana pressure pouring from his body.

Then he sensed a faint pulse of life among the broken branches.

"…Kara?"

He went to her and lifted the young guard into his arms. Blood soaked through her uniform, and she could barely open her eyes.

"Your… Majesty…"

"Don't speak."

"I'm… sorry… I couldn't…"

"Enough."

Lerion's voice grew low. There was no accusation in it.

"You fought with everything you had. Rest now."

When the healers arrived, he placed Kara in their care himself. He watched them carry her out of the forest.

From that day onward, the people of Wavery rarely saw the gentle smile their king had once worn so freely around his family.

---

Somewhere else, Lara opened her eyes.

"…Mother?"

Mio's face was directly above her. Lara's head rested in her mother's lap, and golden sunlight stretched across the stone floor beyond the cave entrance.

"You're awake. Does anything hurt?"

Lara sat bolt upright.

"Where are we, Mother? Where's Father? And Kara? Where is everyone?"

"Easy now." Mio stroked her hair. "I only woke up a little before you. It seems we've ended up in another world."

"Another… world?"

"Mm-hm. I can't see the red sky of the Chaos-Realm anywhere. I think we're in a cave near the sea."

Lara looked around. Water trickled somewhere outside, accompanied by birdsong. She unconsciously closed her hand and realized that the last few flowers were still caught in her palm. They had been crushed almost beyond recognition.

"I…"

Mio looked down at them.

"Oh! Were you picking those for me?"

"Yes. But it's because of me that…"

"Lara."

Mio gave her cheek a gentle pinch.

"Don't take all the blame on yourself. I'm right here with you."

"But Father…"

"I miss him too." Mio paused. "And I believe he's looking for us. Right now, our job is to stay healthy, find a place to live, and wait for a chance to go home."

Lara looked up at her with tear-reddened eyes.

"Do you promise?"

"I promise I'll do everything I can."

Mio rose to her feet and promptly struck her head on the low cave ceiling.

*Bonk!*

"Ow!"

Lara jumped, then burst into laughter. Holding her head, Mio glared indignantly up at the ceiling.

"Well… apparently this cave wasn't built for someone as tall as your mother."

"You're always like this, Mother!"

"That's why I need my princess to lead the way. Come on, let's go outside. We've got to find something to eat first."

---

Outside lay an island with a long beach, dark-green trees, and a sky without the familiar crimson hue. Small waves rolled beneath the warm sunlight. Farther out, the sea stretched so far that it seemed to have no end.

Lara stood motionless, forgetting her hunger for a moment.

"Wow… It's beautiful! Mother, this place is amazing!"

Mio followed the direction her daughter was pointing. She, too, had never seen a sky like this, but the worry of finding their way home remained lodged in her chest.

"All right, then. I suppose it's time I taught my princess a few essential survival skills, hm?"

"Yes!"

And so their life on the island began.

The two found a freshwater stream running down from higher ground, chose a sheltered spot near the forest's edge, wove baskets from vines, and made a place to light fires. At first Lara was clumsy at nearly everything. She released the fishing spear too early, spilled the water she carried back, or forgot to turn a fish until one side was burned black.

Mio patiently showed her how to do it again.

"Now, aim at the right spot, keep your hand steady, and… THROW!"

The homemade spear plunged into the water.

*Splash!*

When Lara pulled it back up, a little fish was caught on its tip.

"HUH?! MOTHER! I DID IT!"

"YAY! Well done, Lara!"

Mio clapped so enthusiastically that Lara nearly dropped her catch laughing. The basket behind them already held enough fish, most of them caught by Mio, to feed them for several meals. Even so, she carefully placed Lara's tiny prize right on top.

At mealtime, she taught her daughter to turn the fish over the glowing coals.

"That's it, nice and evenly. Mind the heat."

Lara blew on a piece and took a tentative bite.

"Mmm! It's pretty good, Mother!"

"Only *pretty* good? I taught you all my secret techniques!"

"I mean… it's delicious!"

"That's more like it!"

Lara scooted closer.

"Did you live like this before you became Queen? You know how to hunt, make a fire, and all sorts of things…"

"I did. There were days when I had to spend morning till night looking for food." Mio broke off a piece of fish, thinking. "So, what do you think about having a mother who doesn't act very royal?"

"I think you're awesome! The coolest!"

Mio laughed and pulled Lara against her shoulder.

But when her daughter was not looking, she touched the calf where the arrow had struck. The pain had eased, but not disappeared. Some days the poison left her hands and feet trembling; on certain mornings she had to remain lying down until the numbness receded.

Then something strange began to happen. The skin around the wound changed, and the damaged flesh repeatedly regenerated. Day by day, the numbness caused by the poison grew weaker. Mio's body was changing so quickly that what had once crippled it no longer affected it in the same way.

That was not entirely good news.

One night, while Lara slept beside the fire, Mio lifted the hem of her clothing and examined her stomach. Hard ridges had appeared beneath her skin, spreading farther than they had the day before. One fingernail had begun to change color and grow unnaturally thick.

"The Mana in this world…"

Mio knew that bodies native to the Chaos-Realm could adapt to harsh environments. But this place was nothing like home. Her body was changing constantly to survive, and if the process continued unchecked, she could not imagine what she might become.

She turned to Lara. The child slept peacefully, although the Mana around her sometimes fluctuated in an unnatural way.

Lara had inherited her mother's capacity to adapt, but she had neither the experience nor the control over Mana needed to protect herself from such changes. Mio laid a hand on her daughter's head and passed some of her own Mana into her, guiding its flow until Lara's body became more stable.

When the fluctuations subsided, Mio exhaled.

The Mana layer that protected her own body had grown a little thinner.

"I'll try again tomorrow, all right?"

She lay down beside her daughter and drew their blanket over them both.

---

One afternoon, Lara sat beside their shelter, studying the flowers she had brought from Wavery. She had tried planting them, watering them, and shielding them from the harshest sunlight, yet the little stems continued to wilt.

Some could no longer hold themselves upright. A few petals had fallen to the earth. The deep-red blossom Lara had kept apart from the others was still clinging to its stem, though its color had begun to fade.

"Why won't they grow…?"

Lara loosened the soil with a twig, then sat staring at it.

Mio approached carrying a basket of fruit she had gathered.

"What's troubling my princess?"

"I've watered them and changed the soil, but they keep wilting. I wanted to keep them so I could give them to you…"

"You already gave them to me."

"But they're all going to die!"

Mio set down the basket and sat beside her. She studied the struggling stems in the unfamiliar earth, then tilted her head.

"Well, you still have one very big flower."

"Where?"

"Why, your mother is the biggest flower you've got!" Mio placed a hand on her chest and straightened proudly. "And this flower even knows how to cook!"

Lara stared at her.

Then she burst out laughing.

"That's cheating, Mother!"

"Cheating? I'd like to see you find another flower that can catch fish as well as I do!"

"There aren't any!"

"Then come hunting with this flower!"

Mio pulled Lara to her feet. The two walked away, still arguing over whether a flower that could cook ought to count as a flower at all. Behind them, a single deep-red petal slowly separated from its blossom and fell onto the freshly turned earth.

---

Several months passed.

Lara could now make a fire without getting smoke in her eyes, distinguish edible fruit from dangerous varieties, and hold her fishing spear more steadily. Sometimes she even prepared food before her mother returned.

But Mio no longer moved as lightly as she had when they first arrived. Her fingernails lengthened, and the skin on her arms and stomach grew hard and rough. Parts of her body changed shape so quickly that she had to learn how to move all over again.

She still used her nails to clean fish. They were sharper than any stone knife, although a careless movement could easily tear something she had never meant to touch.

One evening, Lara sat beside her mother as Mio cut fish into smaller pieces. Her eyes followed Mio's hands—the same hands that had once pinched her cheeks, tied her hair, and held hers on the walk home to the palace.

"Mother…"

"Hm?"

"Does it hurt?"

Mio stopped working.

"Only sometimes. I can still do everything I need to, see?"

"But your hands… and the rest of you…"

Lara lowered her head. She had wanted to ask for days.

"Can we really cure it? You keep changing. I'm afraid that one day…"

Her voice caught.

"I'm afraid you won't recognize me anymore."

Tears fell onto the backs of Lara's hands.

Mio set the fish aside, washed her fingertips, and carefully lifted her daughter's chin with her clawed hand. She angled the hard nails away so that only the softer skin touched Lara's cheek, then gently wiped away her tears.

"Everything will be all right. I'm sure of it."

"But you've been saying that since we first came here…"

"Yes." Mio looked at her daughter. "And I still want to believe it."

She slipped her other arm around Lara's shoulders and drew her close. Lara rested her head against a shoulder that had grown harder than before and listened to the heartbeat inside her mother's chest.

Mio began to sing a lullaby.

It was the same melody she had sung when Lara was very small, on nights when her stubborn little girl refused to go to sleep. Pain occasionally interrupted Mio's voice, but she sang all the way to the end.

Lara's eyelids grew heavy.

"Mother…"

"I'm listening."

"Will you wake me up for breakfast tomorrow?"

"Of course, sweetheart."

"Don't forget…"

"I won't forget."

Lara drifted off to sleep.

Mio remained where she was, letting her daughter rest against her shoulder. When she raised her eyes, she saw the star-filled sky of this unfamiliar world. Then she lowered her head and kissed Lara's hair, very softly, keeping her claws from brushing against her.

Mio stayed like that for a long time that night.

---

In an old notebook she had found on the island, Mio wrote to a husband who might never have the chance to read her words.

*"Lara and I are doing fine. She can catch fish on her own now, and she's even learned how to prepare a few meals. She's growing up so quickly, Lerion."*

She paused and looked at her right hand. Her claws made it harder to hold a pen. Some of her letters came out crooked because she could no longer judge the pressure of her grip.

*"My body is still changing. I've been trying to stabilize Lara's Mana, and she seems to be adapting better. But every time I do it, I find it harder to maintain the Mana that protects my own body."*

*"The poison from the arrow barely affects me anymore. At first I thought that was good news. It's just that my body is becoming something else faster than I can understand it."*

Mio rested her pen for a moment, then continued.

*"I found an old teleportation circle among the stones on the island. Nearby are symbols and instructions written in the language of the Chaos-Realm. Perhaps someone from home came here before us."*

*"I don't know who they were or what they used the circle for. Some parts are broken, but I think I can restore its ability to receive Mana and establish a fixed destination. I'll try. Maybe one day we'll need a way back here in an emergency."*

*"I suppose I should learn to plan ahead like you, shouldn't I?"*

Mio laughed quietly to herself. But when she caught sight of her reflection in the water, her smile slowly faded.

She sat beneath the moonlight and reread what she had written. Other pages were already filled with notes about the changes to her body, Lara's responses to Mana, and Mio's attempts to adjust the flow of energy within her daughter.

Mio understood that she might not have enough time to find a cure. She did not write that on the first page, where Lara might accidentally see it. Instead, she devoted a page near the back to everything she knew, along with instructions for the day her daughter might have to survive without her.

"I wish I could go home," Mio said to the empty sky. "I miss you. I miss everyone so much…"

She wiped her eyes and looked toward Lara, who was sleeping nearby.

"I'm sorry, Lara."

Mio had once worried that she didn't know enough to teach her daughter the etiquette expected of a princess. She was not as skilled as Lerion at discussing affairs of state, nor did she have the palace's books and tutors to help Lara study Magic. Now she was no longer sure she had enough time to pass on even the things she did know.

A tear fell and touched Lara's cheek.

"…Mother?"

The girl stirred and opened her eyes.

"Why aren't you asleep? Are you crying?"

Mio hurriedly wiped her face.

"Ah… I just had a little nightmare."

"But you were sitting here writing."

"Well…" Mio hesitated, then sighed. "I miss home."

Lara sat up and wrapped her arms around her mother's waist, carefully avoiding the patches of skin that had changed.

"I miss it too. But I'm still here with you."

Mio set the notebook aside and held her daughter close.

"I love you so much."

"I love you too, Mother."

This time, Mio did not look for an excuse to hide her tears. She simply held Lara until the girl fell asleep again.

---

As Lara gradually adapted to the Mana of the new world, she became capable of attempting the techniques her mother used to stabilize the body. Mio taught her step by step, patiently explaining how to sense the flow of Mana, how to guide it in the right direction, and how to prevent it from dispersing before it could take effect.

But understanding the instructions was not the same as putting them into practice.

On the first day, Lara's Mana dispersed almost as soon as she gathered it. Over the next several days, she learned to hold it a little longer, but never long enough to slow the changes in Mio's body.

"Let's try again," Mio said.

"I already have!"

"Then rest for a while, and try again afterward."

"But you don't have much time left!"

The words burst out before Lara could stop them. Both fell silent.

Lara looked down. She knew she had raised her voice, but the harder she tried to hold herself together, the more her lips trembled. After nearly two weeks of training, she still could not do what her mother accomplished with a single touch.

"I'm sorry…"

She bit her lip until it hurt. What made Lara angriest was not simply that the lesson was difficult, but that she had nowhere to direct her helpless frustration. Her hands had grown sharper claws and strange patches of skin had formed behind her ear, yet her control over Mana was still nowhere near what she needed.

Mio held out her hand.

"Come here."

Lara hesitated, then stepped closer. Her mother embraced her against her chest. The skin there was rough now, as hard as scales, but when Lara pressed her ear to it, she could still hear the familiar heartbeat.

*Thump. Thump.*

The girl closed her eyes, slowing her breathing to match the rhythm.

"I'll learn," Lara said, her voice still thick with tears. "If I can't cure you myself, I'll find someone who can help. I'm not giving up."

"Mm-hm."

Mio stroked her hair.

"Then I'll teach you everything I can still remember."

---

Seven months had passed since mother and daughter arrived on the island.

There was no longer any way to hide the changes to Mio's body. Hard patches of skin spread farther, her limbs grew larger, and her figure changed until it was difficult to recognize the woman who had once stood in Wavery's kitchen. Her voice grew coarse and hoarse. Even short answers became more difficult with each passing day.

Some mornings, Lara would call to her and Mio would take a long time to turn around. Once, she held a fish in her hand but could not remember what she was supposed to do with it. Lara walked over, touched her mother's wrist, and gently guided her through the movements Mio herself had taught her.

Mio looked at her daughter and struggled to make a sound that was almost an answer. Then she rested her enormous hand on Lara's head.

So gently that the tips of her claws barely touched the girl's skin.

Lara began taking charge of hunting, cooking, repairing their sleeping place, and caring for her mother. She kept trying the Mana-adjustment techniques she had learned, remembering even her failures, but she could not stop Mio's transformation.

One day she returned carrying a basket full of fish and several ripe red fruits.

"Mother! Look how much I caught today!"

Mio turned. A scaled hand slowly rose and stroked her daughter's hair. Only a few low growls came from her throat.

"We'll be all right. We still have each other, don't we, Mother?"

The hand continued to stroke Lara's hair.

The girl looked down at her own hands. Her nails had grown long and sharp, and even her teeth were different. Rough scales had appeared near one ear. Although her body was adapting more steadily now, those changes had not disappeared.

Lara sat down and leaned against her mother's enormous body.

After a while, she whispered,

"Everything… will be all right."

---

Then a full year had passed.

Mio could scarcely speak anymore. What remained of her responses came mostly from instinct, habit, and the attention she still gave Lara. Yet Lara could recognize her mother in the smallest things: she always turned toward her daughter's voice, always gave Lara the driest place to sleep, and never once stopped shielding her when dangerous animals came near.

Lara's body was still changing, though far more slowly than her mother's. If they remained on the island, she had no idea whether they would find a cure before it was too late.

She had read Mio's notes over and over. She knew her mother had tried to repair the old teleportation circle and create a return route to the island, but the missing parts of the structure were beyond Lara's abilities. It had never been capable of opening a path to Wavery, either.

The girl closed the notebook and sat beside her mother for a long time.

"Mother… I want to find someone who can help."

Mio tilted her head. Lara could find no clear answer in those eyes, so different from how they had once been. But her mother lowered herself, allowing Lara to climb onto her, just as she had done when helping her daughter through the deeper water around the island.

Lara gripped the scales on her mother's shoulder.

"I won't go far from you."

Mio stepped into the water.

And their journey began.

---

The sea was deeper than Lara had ever imagined.

Mio did not swim on the surface like the fish they used to catch. Her body had become so heavy and solid that remaining afloat was difficult. Instead, she walked step by step over the rocky seabed, allowing her own weight to draw her down after each journey to the surface.

Her affinity for water and ice Mana still functioned, even though her mind was no longer clear enough to wield it as she once had. The water around Lara parted, leaving a small pocket of air enclosed within a thin layer of Mana. The pressure of the ocean remained outside it, allowing Lara to sit upon her mother's broad shoulders and breathe.

But the air inside that pocket was not endless.

Whenever Lara began to feel short of breath, Mio stopped. She grasped protruding rocks and pushed herself slowly upward with her changing body. Sometimes it took a very long time to reach the surface. Mio would emerge among the waves, renew the air around her daughter, and then allow herself to sink again.

At first, her movements were so awkward that Lara had to cling tightly to the scales on her neck. Sometimes Mio collided with rocks, clouding the water all around them. But with each stage of the journey, her body changed again. Parts exposed to the greatest pressure grew thicker and harder, and movements that had once been difficult gradually became less clumsy.

They passed sharp outcrops, deep crevices, and stretches of water so dark that Lara could not see her own hand beyond the faint glow of Mana. At one break in the seabed, Mio had to follow the cliff face for a long time before finding a place where she could descend.

Lara lost track of how many nights had passed. When they surfaced for air, sometimes she saw stars; at other times, the sky held nothing but gray clouds above towering waves.

"Mother, where are we going?"

Mio answered with a low rumble almost swallowed by the water.

Lara hugged her tighter.

Several days later, Mio suddenly changed course. She stopped on a stretch of flat rock, lifted her head toward a different current, and began walking faster. Something far away had caught her instincts' attention.

Lara squinted into the dark water. She could see nothing yet, but for the first time in days, her mother seemed to be heading toward a place where living creatures could be found.

When they next emerged above the waves, the horizon was no longer empty.

There was land ahead.

**Seawind.**

---

Lara pulled up her cloak to cover her hair and the ear that had begun to change. She did not want strangers to see her claws, sharp teeth, or patches of scales and become frightened. Although she was sitting on the shoulders of a gigantic creature far more fearsome than herself, she still took care to straighten her hood.

"Mother, there are people here. Maybe they'll be able to help us…"

Mio approached the shore. Deep rumbles escaped her throat whenever a large wave struck her body, but Lara patiently patted her hardened shoulder.

On land, the first people to notice them stopped what they were doing.

A gigantic creature, covered in tough skin and armed with claws, was emerging from the sea toward their homes. High above it sat a small hooded figure between the scales, occasionally bending down as though giving the monster instructions.

Shouts of alarm spread along the shore.

"Please, wait!" Lara called, waving both arms. "We don't want to hurt anyone! Please, my mother needs help!"

No one answered in a language she understood.

People on the shore shouted to one another, pointing at the enormous creature and then at the hooded figure on its shoulders. Lara tried repeating herself, gesturing that she was unarmed and only wanted to talk. But the distance, the crashing waves, and the barrier of language rendered every attempt useless.

A few adventurers were among those who came running. One young man drew his bow, looking ready to step in front of the crowd and distinguish himself. Then Mio lowered her head, her vast shadow falling across the water, and his hands began to tremble.

He stepped back.

Then turned and fled.

Others began moving the townspeople into shelters and bringing out weapons to defend the waterfront. Lara watched them in growing panic.

"Mother, maybe we shouldn't go any closer. Let's leave. We'll find somewhere else…"

Mio stopped. She glanced down at her daughter, but before she could turn away, a faint sound came from the rocks farther along the shore.

*Whoosh!*

The young adventurer had not truly left. He was hiding some distance away, in a place where his fear had subsided but his humiliation at running away still burned. From there, he drew his bow and aimed at the hooded figure on the monster's shoulders.

The arrow came too quickly for Lara to dodge.

It struck the hard scales that had spread from her ear across one cheek, bounced away, and fell into the sea. But the scales cracked. A small piece broke away from her skin, leaving a red streak of blood running down her neck.

Lara touched her face and stared blankly at the blood on her fingertips.

"Mother…?"

Mio had sensed the hostile intent even before the arrow reached her daughter. Now the scent of Lara's blood made her snap her head upward. A roar erupted from her throat like a wave crashing straight into the shore.

"NO, MOTHER!"

Lara clung to her shoulder.

"I'm all right! Mother, I'm all right!"

But Mio was no longer clear-minded enough to understand. In the fragments of awareness that remained, the creatures ahead had aimed weapons at Lara and injured her daughter.

That was enough.

She charged.

Water surged upward, scattering objects along the waterfront. Coastal structures shook beneath Mio's enormous weight. Cries for help echoed through the area, followed by volleys of Magic and arrows from the people defending their homes.

Amid the violent movement, Lara lost her balance and fell from her mother's shoulder into the water below.

"MOTHER! STOP!"

She tried to swim closer, but Mio had already turned toward a gathering of armed defenders. She knocked aside attacks and destroyed whatever blocked her path. Waves and flying debris hurled some people to the ground. Others helped the injured retreat while continuing to fight, because their homes and families were still behind them.

Lara kept calling, gesturing, trying to make her mother understand that not everyone here was hunting them.

It was no use.

A stray spell flew toward her. Mio immediately turned and placed herself between it and her daughter, letting the flash of Magic strike the scales on her back.

Then it happened again.

And again.

"Mother…"

Lara watched wounds open across that enormous body. Most closed quickly as Mana continued to surge through Mio. But whenever an attack came near Lara, her mother turned to protect her, no matter where she had been fighting.

"If only… if it weren't for me…"

The girl clenched her clawed hands. The blood on her cheek was beginning to dry, but the wound still stung.

On the shore, a child was crying for a family member. Someone was trying to drag a wounded person out from beneath fallen timber. The shouts came in words Lara did not understand, but she knew the people were frightened.

They were protecting the place they lived.

Her mother was protecting her.

And no one could hear anyone else's pleas.

"Father…" Lara whispered. "Kara…"

She stared at the foaming white waves, her tears falling into the sea.

"I wish you were all here…"

No answer came from the shore. Only the collisions of Magic, Mio's roars, and voices calling for people to flee the fighting.

Lara looked up. Her mother was still there, inside a body that had become utterly unlike the one she remembered. The hard scales protecting her chest rose and fell with every breath.

Amid the chaos, Lara heard another sound—deep, familiar.

*Thump.*

*Thump.*

Mio's heart was still beating.

Once, Lara had often fallen asleep against her mother's shoulder while listening to her lullabies. Later, even after Mio's body began to change, she had leaned against that chest whenever she needed to calm herself after another failed Mana lesson.

It was the same heartbeat.

The same person who had promised to wake her for breakfast.

"Mother…"

Lara began to cry.

"Please, stop!"

She looked toward the shore, where the townspeople were still fighting back.

"Please, everyone… please stop fighting!"

Her voice vanished in the wind.

"PLEASE, I'M BEGGING YOU—"

A sharp sound cut through her plea.

*Whoosh!*

Lara could not follow its path. An attack punched through the thick, hardened defenses at the front of Mio's chest, striking the one place none of the earlier attacks had been able to reach. The sea exploded upward behind her.

Mio staggered.

Her other wounds were still closing, but the one at the center of her chest did not heal. The Mana that sustained her massive body suddenly faltered and scattered. Deep inside her rib cage, the core bound to her life had been pierced.

*Thump…*

Lara waited for the next beat.

It never came.

"Mother…?"

Mio collapsed into the water. The attacks around her slowed as her gigantic body fell. Lara rushed to her, struggling to embrace a face that was now larger than her entire body.

"MOTHER!"

Although scales, claws, and layers of hardened skin had made that face almost unrecognizable, Lara still knew it was Mio.

A weak voice escaped her mother's throat.

"Lara…"

The girl jerked her head up.

For a moment, clarity returned to Mio's clouded eyes. Not much—just enough for her to see her daughter's face and the blood still marking her cheek.

"I'm… sorry…"

"NO! Don't say that!" Lara shook her head through her tears. "Everything will be all right! We'll be all right! You promised!"

She pressed both hands against her mother's body, trying to guide her own Mana into the thick scales. She had practiced doing this so many times. Sometimes she had managed a little; sometimes nothing at all. But now, the moment her Mana reached Mio's chest, it dispersed. Nothing she did could bring back the heartbeat that had stopped.

"Please…"

Lara bit her lip and tried again.

"I learned how… You taught me how…"

Nothing happened.

Mio no longer had the strength to move. She looked at her daughter, and the last of her Mana answered a thought that had stayed with her throughout their time on the island: keep Lara away from danger.

Seawater gathered around the girl, mingling with the tears still on Mio's cheeks, and formed a transparent cocoon. Lara pressed her hands against the water in alarm.

"Mother…?"

Far away, the old teleportation circle among the stones on their island began to shine.

One by one, the symbols Mio had patiently repaired over so many months received the Mana she had prepared to activate them. The circle had never been capable of opening a path back to Wavery. Mio had restored only part of its structure, transforming it into an anchor that could receive someone returning to this very place.

An emergency route.

It needed to work only once.

Mio looked at Lara one last time. The girl was still reaching toward her, unable to understand why the water was carrying her away.

"MOTHER! I DON'T WANT TO GO!"

Her fingertips barely brushed the scales on Mio's hand.

Then the space around Lara twisted.

She disappeared.

---

When Lara opened her eyes, she was lying inside the teleportation circle on the familiar island.

The water surrounding her dissolved, running down between the stones. The circle flickered a few times before its light began to fade. The symbols and freshly repaired carvings Mio had worked on were still there, joining the damaged sections of the original structure.

Lara sat up and reached out to touch them.

"…Mother managed to fix it…"

She remembered her mother visiting this place again and again, sometimes returning very late with stone dust and ink on her fingertips. She remembered the pages of her journal covered in unfamiliar symbols. She remembered Mio saying that they needed another way back in case of an emergency.

Not a path to Wavery.

Not a miracle capable of saving both of them.

Only a place where they knew how to find food, fresh water, and shelter. A place where Lara had learned her first lessons in surviving this strange world, and somewhere her mother could send her without needing to find any other coordinates.

Lara looked down at her hands. Her nails were still sharper than before. Hard scales remained around her ear and along one cheek, some of them cracked. She did not know how to return to Wavery, nor how to stop her body from changing further.

But the thing that frightened her most now was no longer her appearance.

There was no enormous figure waiting for her to bring home fish.

No clawed hand trying so carefully to rest upon her head.

No one to remind her that sleeping too long meant missing a meal.

"…Mother?"

Waves broke against the rocky shore.

"Mother…"

No one answered.

Lara drew her knees to her chest and sobbed on the island that mother and daughter had once called their shelter. She cried until she no longer had the strength to call out again.

Behind her, the water remaining among the stones suddenly stirred.

The last droplets from Lara's protective cocoon slowly gathered together. Mio's Mana still lingered within them, carrying the final message she had placed there before she died.

A familiar figure appeared.

It was not Mio returned from the dead. Only an image preserved in water Magic, so fragile that light passed straight through its hands.

The figure knelt behind Lara and wrapped its arms around her from behind—gently, like the nights beside the fire.

"I love you."

Lara froze.

She knew those arms held none of their former warmth, but she turned toward the voice anyway.

"…I love you too, Mother."

Mio's image kept smiling as it slowly dissolved into tiny motes of light. The last traces of Mana followed the water into the earth, leaving only a few glistening droplets on Lara's hands.

The sun had set without her noticing. Above her, the moon climbed higher into the sky of this unfamiliar world.

Lara remained there for a long time. When her sobbing finally faded into ragged breaths, she wiped her face and slowly pushed herself to her feet.

Behind her, the teleportation circle had gone completely dark. It could not take her back to Seawind, nor could it open a path to Wavery.

It had fulfilled the final purpose Mio had given it.

To bring her daughter home.
